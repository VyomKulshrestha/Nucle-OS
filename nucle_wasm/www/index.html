How the recovery estimate works. Each trial writes the sample, simulates synthesis (Twist, for the sequencer profiles) followed by 10 independent reads per strand, votes a consensus for every strand, polishes it to the codec's strand length, and runs Reed-Solomon on the result. The percentage is the share of trials that return the exact input. With the default sample and the ternary codec, Illumina recovered in every trial and Nanopore, at roughly 7% per-base error, in 9 of 10, at both 2 and 6 parity strands. On real MinION reads (Microsoft's Clustered Nanopore Reads dataset) the same decoder reproduces 94.7% of strands exactly; nucle reads runs that check.

What this models. Every backbone bond breaks at a rate that rises with temperature (the Arrhenius law), and a strand with any break cannot be amplified, so it is lost. A sequence is gone once all of its physical copies have broken; the file is gone once more sequences are lost than its Reed-Solomon parity can rebuild. The kinetics are published measurements, but they were taken at 45–140 °C and extrapolated down, so figures in the millions of years are what the fitted rate implies, not something anyone has tested. nucle shelf-life and NucleScript's retain: requirement use the same model.
